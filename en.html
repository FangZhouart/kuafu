Project

Overview

One streamer, one wild man,
and a chase about being watched.

I Am Not Kua Fu is a VR immersive narrative game whose only narrative interface is a first-person livestream. You put on a headset and are dropped straight into Xiao K's stream — an outdoor shoot in a forest in 2050, until a mysterious "wild man" walks into the frame. From then on, everything that happens is watched by the platform, the audience and the algorithm at once.

It continues the VR method built in Chinese Immortals: that 14-minute piece used 20,000 photographs to scan folk shrines and let the audience walk among them. Here the audience is put inside a machine that is already broadcasting — the headset is the machine, the lens is the player's eye.

It begins with an ordinary shoot. Xiao K rides a motorbike through the hills chasing a wild man that keeps growing: you type a signature catchphrase yourself, you copy its gestures, and voice and movement establish a resonance between you. But when the sun comes back out the resonance breaks — it only follows the sun, until on the coast it unfolds into the giant who "descends as Kua Fu".

You then switch to Agent B, tasked with stopping the creature code-named Kua Fu before it reaches the city: kill, or wound? The body part you hit changes everything that follows. Once subdued, Kua Fu is taken to a containment centre converted from a vast dry dock. The military and an entertainment conglomerate argue over its commercial value; the compromise is to let it do one trial broadcast. Xiao K, now a major influencer, becomes its "admin".

After that you decide whether to make Kua Fu perform something grotesque and exaggerated on camera. That choice opens four endings: global stardom, total containment, a failed escape, or a return to the forest — where it fades away as the sun goes down.

Kua Fu chasing the sun is a myth about pursuing something unreachable. In the game, what is chased becomes traffic, spectacle and other people's attention.

The streamer chases a trending topic, the platform chases retention, the audience chases the next broadcast. The myth is not retold — its roles are reassigned. Every viewer takes part in Kua Fu's growth.

02

Story

Five movements
CH.01

Prologue · The Chase

A forest livestream, and a wild man walks into frame. Xiao K rides out after it; you type a catchphrase, you copy its gestures, and the resonance slows it down. Then the sun breaks through, the resonance snaps, and it goes back to chasing the sun — until, on the coast, it unfolds into the giant that "descends as Kua Fu". The level turns from a chase into a mythological event.

CH.02

Control

B is sent to stop a creature code-named Kua Fu from entering the city. Kill it, or wound it? Which part you hit decides what it can still do afterwards.

CH.03

The Argument

The military and an entertainment conglomerate argue over whether to keep squeezing commercial value out of it. The compromise: one trial broadcast. Xiao K becomes Kua Fu's admin.

CH.04

Broadcast

The first broadcast. You decide whether to make Kua Fu perform something grotesque and exaggerated for the camera — and that decides which road you take.

Four Endings

5.1 — 5.4
5.1

Entertainment to Death

Traffic above all; the broadcast outperforms every forecast. The two sides sign a long-term deal: retire the real Kua Fu and put a virtual one on stage. In the farewell show it is hit by a rocket from the chat and EMOJI spill out of its body; in reality it is anaesthetised and driven away.

To see what really happened, remember how you put the MR glasses on in the prologue — and reverse it.
5.2

Learned Helplessness

The broadcast fails and Kua Fu is contained by the military. B can't bear it and conspires with Xiao K to set it free. But if Xiao K has been feeding it on schedule every day, then on the night of the escape it hears the dinner bell and sits down to eat.

The better you control it, the less you can save it.
5.3

So Close

The escape actually begins. B and Xiao K must work together to get it out of the dry dock. One mistake and both of them are arrested.

Two points of view, one body.
5.4

Back to the Wild

The two lead it back to the old forest. As the sun goes down, Kua Fu fades away. The plan works.

The price is turning it back into a legend.

MR GlassesMR 眼镜

Augmented-reality glasses from a future entertainment company: the same thing looks different to different people. The essential device of the "everything livestream".

Kua Fu夸父

A humanoid creature whose size changes with sunlight. Some research suggests its size may also track how much attention humans pay to it.

Resonance共振

Affected by Xiao K's voice, Kua Fu can't help imitating his movements when it hears certain tones. Direct sunlight breaks the link, and it turns back to the sun.

03

World & Characters

Design

Character design starts from orthographic sheets. Kua Fu is a huge body under pale fur with a single antler; but the face is not a god's — brow, beard and lowered eyes belong to a tired middle-aged man. Divinity and embarrassment share one face.

Xiao K grew out of the livestream: bleached buzz cut, tiger-print tee, tattooed arms. He is not the hero; he is the one who found the hero.

The containment centre is a converted dry dock — industrial scale overlapping mythological scale. Maps and staging were blocked out in sketches before any 3D was built.

04

Interface & Process

Method

The streaming HUD is not decoration; it is how the player understands the world. Viewer counts, gifts, danger warnings, navigation — every piece of information is a cue and a pressure at once. The busier the interface, the harder it is to tell whether you are operating, or being operated. In VR it is no longer a layer in front of your eyes but a space around you: danmaku drift past your ear, gifts explode overhead.

So the interface is rebuilt from the information hierarchy of real livestream products: danmaku on the left, gifts and rankings on the right, status along the top, input at the bottom. In the end it belongs to no existing platform — only to this world that has been swallowed by being watched.

Storyboards run on two tracks: hand-drawn boards lock the staging and the mood, image generation previews light, texture and lens, and the 3D build settles it. The hesitation of the drawing and the over-clarity of the render are both kept, until they resolve into a single light.

He holds a BA in Product Design and an MA in Photography from the Central Academy of Fine Arts, where he is now a doctoral candidate in Fine Arts, researching the language of moving images and new media, and the relation between traditional and contemporary visual art.

His recent work keeps moving toward VR: Chinese Immortals scanned shrines from 20,000 photographs, and I Am Not Kua Fu puts the audience inside a world that is already broadcasting. His practice spans photography, digital imaging and new media, exploring the collisions, transformations and fusions between traditional Chinese culture and modern civilisation. His work has been shown at the Annecy International Animation Film Festival, the Chengdu Contemporary Image Museum and the Seoul Independent Animation Festival, among others. Awards include Best VR Film at Aesthetica Film Festival (UK), the Kunpeng Award at the Pingyao International Photography Festival, and honours from the Asian Pioneer Photographer Awards, Student World Impact Film Festival, the 1839 Photography Award and The Alpine Fellowship.
